Eighth revision: stop calling the nested quantities intervals, withdraw the
cluster bootstrap over factorial design points, and lead with the range the
outer counts can actually determine

The first comment was that the variance-deflated interval is not a validated
95% patient-level interval and should not be used as one. The shrinkage
matches a second moment; it does not in general recover the quantiles of the
patient-sampling distribution from the convolution of that distribution with
Monte Carlo noise, and these statistics are bounded proportions produced by
a procedure containing a Shapley sign step and a frontier selection, neither
smooth. We had said coverage was not established and then reasoned from
whether an interval covered one half, which is using an uncalibrated
interval as a formal inferential tool. The reviewer offered two remedies and
preferred the second at our compute; so do we.

These quantities are now PATIENT-RESAMPLED RANGES throughout. The phrases
"95% interval", "confidence interval" and "patient-level interval" appear
only where they are being disclaimed. Every conclusion that turned on where
a range endpoint fell is gone: from the abstract, from the matched
comparison, from the paired rule comparison, from the Discussion and
Conclusions and from the table notes. What replaces them is a statement of
how wide the range is and that it straddles the value in question, with no
claim about any population value.

A non-positive v_total - v_mc now reads as the patient-level variance
component being NOT IDENTIFIABLE at this Monte Carlo precision. Earlier
revisions read it first as the quantity being nearly constant and then as
the inner term accounting for the whole spread. Both are withdrawn in the
Results, in Table S3 and in Table S3c, each of which says so.

The cluster bootstrap over the sixteen factorial scenarios is withdrawn.
They are the fixed design points of a resolution-IV fractional factorial,
not clusters drawn from a population of mechanisms; resampling them with
replacement had no sampling population to refer to, destroyed the factorial
balance, and because the quartile edges were recut inside each replicate did
not resample the same statistic. Table S2c now carries a leave-one-scenario-
out sensitivity analysis under the fixed design, and the claim is the
reviewer's: the three chosen descriptive summaries were numerically similar
under this particular binning scheme. The table adds that omitting one
scenario reverses the sign of the small events-minus-patients difference, so
that sign carries no information.

At 150 and 100 outer replicates the 2.5 and 97.5 points rest on the fourth
and third replicate from each end. The central 80% range now leads
everywhere, its endpoints resting on the fifteenth and tenth; the wider span
is kept but printed to one decimal, and the Monte Carlo error of both is
reported. The counts behind the ranges are 149 and 99, not the nominal 150
and 100, and that is now disclosed.

Table S1e no longer says it licenses the deflation. It closes with what it
does not establish: it runs on colon only, so the same correction on the
Rotterdam ranges is unvalidated; several of its observed spreads rest on two
to five blocks; flat block means are arithmetic rather than an independent
test of unbiasedness; and a variance law says nothing about the coverage of
a linearly shrunken quantile.

Minors: "too wide" becomes "carries inner Monte Carlo variance that patient
sampling alone would not", since with coverage unknown we cannot promise the
observed range is conservative. "Accepted in full" is replaced by a
statement of what was and was not done. The Results subheading matches the
title. Table precision matches the reported endpoint errors, including an
admission that the second decimal of the central range is not determined
either and is kept only to keep narrow ranges distinguishable.

Found by our own audit of the redraft, and reported in the letter:

- A Methods sentence comparing three propensity weightings still called a
  range an interval, printed it to two decimals and concluded from the fact
  that it contained all three estimates. The conclusion was empty as well:
  the estimates differ by 0.018 and the range is 0.65 wide.
- The replacement wording was itself an over-claim. "Moves across most of
  the distance between chance and certainty" is false of a range about a
  third of a unit wide, and it concealed that the range extends below one
  half as well as above. Two further sentences said the ranges reached "well
  past" one half and zero, where the margins are about 0.05; those read as
  the tests we had just deleted.
- The leading claim's arithmetic was wrong: at 150 replicates the tenth
  percentile is the fifteenth order statistic, not the tenth.
- Table S1e still carried a coverage claim ("the anti-conservative of the
  two") fifty lines above the paragraph saying the table cannot speak to
  coverage, resting on an observed-to-implied ratio range of 0.7 to 1.0 when
  the table's own cells run 0.6 to 2.5.
- The Methods counted four interval procedures including the coefficient
  intervals of the interval-censored regression; that table prints no
  coefficients and no intervals. The count is three.
- Table S3's deflated ranges can sit wholly to one side of the point
  estimate beside them, because they are centred on the bootstrap's own
  noisier mean. That is now explained in the note rather than left to look
  like an error.
- The dash means different things in Table S1e and in Tables S3 and S3c;
  each now says which.
- Table S1e was never cited in the manuscript. It is now.
