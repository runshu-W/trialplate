Fourth revision: one primary analysis, a corrected Rotterdam endpoint, and softer claims

The review began from a main-text / supplement inconsistency that the previous
response had explained away as Monte Carlo noise. It was not. Eleven analysis
scripts specified the Rotterdam endpoint as ("rtime", "death") at tau = 1825 --
the relapse-free TIME paired with the DEATH indicator, which is not a valid pair
and systematically shortens event times -- while rule_eval.R, behind Table 1,
correctly used ("dtime", "death") at tau = 2555. All eleven are corrected and
every affected number is rebuilt.

Structural fix so it cannot recur:
- analysis/primary.R (new) produces every observed-cohort point estimate quoted
  anywhere in the paper, in one pass per cohort, into one result file. The
  abstract, main text, Table 1 and supplement all read from it; the nested
  bootstrap now supplies uncertainty only.
- export_numbers.R asserts on the exported numbers (endpoint names, both
  horizons, primary-vs-nested agreement) and scans the source of all 48 analysis
  scripts, refusing to export if any line pairs rtime with death or calls a
  Rotterdam dataset at the colon horizon.

New analyses:
- analysis/threeway.R  fit / select / test thirds, measuring frontier optimism
  inside the real cohorts. 35.3% of colon dominators and 41.4% of Rotterdam
  dominators survive on untouched patients; frontier membership moves only
  2.5% -> 4.3%.
- analysis/horizon_sweep.R  the restricted-mean horizon rationale, criterion-set
  agreement at four horizons per cohort, and the finding that the sign of the
  absolute-benefit comparison in colon depends on the horizon.
- analysis/snr_curve.R  success against a criterion-specific signal-to-noise
  ratio: Spearman 0.86 against 0.45 for fitting size, over 12 cells, reported as
  a direction rather than an answer.

nested_all.R: the inner Monte Carlo variance is now estimated from the sample
variance of the per-split values within each resample instead of p(1-p)/R, which
was correct only for the 0/1 statistics; matched-statistic intervals widen
accordingly. Interval-endpoint convergence is reported for every quantity (max
movement 0.040 colon, 0.047 Rotterdam between half and the full outer count).
The RMST-selected rule variant is carried through the same loop, so all of its
out-of-sample metrics now have patient-level intervals.

Claims withdrawn or softened:
- "The effect-estimate promise was not kept in either cohort" -> the observed
  proportions were below one half, but patient-level uncertainty establishes
  neither failure nor success.
- "Switching the estimand gains nothing" -> point estimates no higher on either
  estimand, intervals too wide to settle it.
- The sentence naming the largest diluting coefficient as the governing quantity
  is deleted; our own results contradict it.
- "Read the requirement off conditional curves" is removed and Table 2 is
  labelled a sensitivity analysis.
- "Validation" -> "out-of-sample evaluation" in the title, abstract and keywords.

Also: Supplement Text S1.4 factor level 10 -> 8; "between-cohort" -> 
"outer-sampling" throughout; trailing whitespace removed; page breaks tightened
(main 20pp, supplement 10pp, no blank pages); Chinese explainer rewritten to the
new conclusions at 3 pages.
