Fifth revision: enforce the single-source claim with a build guard, and fix what it found

The fourth cover letter claimed every figure in the paper is generated from one
result file. That claim was false, and the reviewer was right to test it. Results
section 2 quoted two probabilities as typed literals; the Methods quoted three
weighting probabilities the same way; and two superseded result objects were still
being read by the supplement, one of them produced before the Rotterdam endpoint
correction. A claim of that kind has to be enforced by the build.

manuscript/check_numbers.js (new) scans every double-quoted string in all five
document sources and fails the build on any decimal or percentage that is not a
whitelisted design constant. It runs as the first step of build.js. On first run it
found 84 transcribed figures, far more than the review identified.

What the guard found, beyond the reviewer's list:
- a Figure 4 caption claiming the estimator's population limit is inflated by 32%
  under unmeasured confounding; recomputed from the script actually in the
  repository it is 8.6%, and 32% came from a parameterisation that no longer exists
- the leverage over-prediction for the OR and XOR forms stated as 10-30%; it is
  11-44%
- Supplement Tables S1, S1b, S1c and S2b typed by hand
- Figure 3 plotting the pre-correction Rotterdam values while reading its intervals
  from a retired result object
- Table S1c dividing an already-normalised truncation fraction by n a second time
- "its Shapley value is negative throughout", contradicted by the zero-heterogeneity
  cell it cites, which is positive
- a missing Figure 2: the main text ran 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 after a figure was dropped

Structural changes:
- five superseded result objects moved to analysis/out/superseded/ with a note on
  why each was retired; export_numbers.R refuses to run if any reappears
- Supplement Table S3c deleted; the RMST-rule variant is reported once, in Table S3
- strat_split() moved into analysis/_setup.R so every script shares one split
  function; the weighting sensitivity and the horizon sweep now run at the primary
  analysis's seed and split count, and export_numbers.R asserts their overlapping
  cells reproduce the primary values exactly
- one interval procedure for the whole paper: the 95% percentile interval of the
  outer bootstrap distribution. No interval is formed from a standard deviation
  multiplier
- confound_limits.R (new) computes the three population gap limits that had been
  typed into both figures.R and the Results

Estimands separated, per major point 3:
The three-way split conflated a replication rate with a fresh scan of the test
third. They are now three named quantities, and only the second is confirmatory.
This changes a headline: judged against comparator subsets fixed on separate data
before evaluation, the rule is left undominated in 21.0% of colon and 12.0% of
Rotterdam splits, against 3.0% and 1.0% on the empirical reading. The qualitative
finding survives, but quoting only the empirical figure overstates the shortfall by
a factor of several. The abstract, Results, Discussion and Conclusions now carry
both, with the confirmatory one identified as the out-of-sample statement.

Claims corrected:
- the RMST-selected variant is no longer described as "not higher on either
  estimand", which its own point estimates contradict; it shows no clear or
  consistent advantage, with intervals establishing neither superiority nor
  equivalence
- the leftover "Table 2 read as a family of conditional reliability curves"
  sentence is deleted
- the pooled SNR correlation is reported with its within- and between-arrangement
  decomposition, since the pooled figure is confounded by construction
- the 0.05 margin is called an investigator-chosen sensitivity margin throughout
- Hamming distance is out of nine criteria, not six

Verification: a clean rebuild from the stored result objects through to the three
documents, with the guard and assertion output, is in manuscript/clean_build_log.txt.
Rerunning primary.R reproduced every previously reported value bit for bit in both
cohorts. The guard does not test bare integers; those were checked by hand.
