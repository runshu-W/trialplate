The first reviewer comment is the sharpest statistical criticism this paper
has had. The nested bootstrap draws B outer resamples and takes R inner
splits inside each, so the quantity it resamples is an R-split estimator
while the reported point estimates use 400 or 500 splits. Its percentile
interval therefore carries the inner Monte Carlo variance on top of the
population variance and is too wide by that amount. We had recorded the
symptom -- the bootstrap's own observed-data replicate differs from the
point estimate by up to 0.14 -- and left a sentence standing in Text S1.6
saying the two could not disagree. Both cannot be true.

What the revision does about it.

analysis/split_converge.R (new) runs the observed-data estimator at a
ladder of split counts on the colon cohort, using each design's own
point-estimate seed and split count so the table is a prefix of the
reported run and its largest column reproduces the reported point rather
than contradicting it. It reports the per-split spread, the implied Monte
Carlo standard deviation s/sqrt(R), and -- this is the part that carries
the argument -- the observed standard deviation and mean of independent
R-split estimates formed by cutting the long run into disjoint blocks.
s/sqrt(R) alone is an identity once s is fixed and is evidence for
nothing; the block statistics are observed, and the flatness of the block
means in R is the empirical form of the fact that these estimators are
averages over independent splits, so the split count changes their
variance and not their expectation. The difference between the bootstrap's
statistic and the reported one is a variance difference, which deflation
can address, not a bias, which it could not.

analysis/export_numbers.R now produces, for every out-of-sample
probability, a deflated percentile interval alongside the raw one: each
outer replicate is shrunk toward the mean by sqrt((v_tot - v_mc)/v_tot)
before the percentiles are taken. Where the estimated inner variance is at
least the total spread the outer component is not estimable, and the
tables print a dash rather than a degenerate point. It also reports the
Monte Carlo standard error of the 2.5% and 97.5% endpoints themselves,
obtained by resampling the outer replicates -- in the note to Table S3 and
in a row of its own in Table S3c, which is drawn from the smaller outer
count and is where the comment bites hardest.

The claims are downgraded to match. The abstract and Methods say the
intervals are approximate, that they carry inner Monte Carlo variance, and
that their coverage against a known population is not established; the
Shapley sign step is a non-smooth selection step for which we would not
assume nominal coverage. "Confirmatory" is withdrawn from the manuscript
and the supplement. The three-way comparison is against the selection
third of the same design rather than against half-split figures that also
change the size of the evaluation set.

The second comment concerns the information-currency comparison. Sixteen
scenarios binned into quartiles cannot establish that patients, events and
effective sample size are equivalent, and we no longer write as though
they could. New Table S2c gives the bin edges, the rows and distinct
scenarios in each bin, the within-bin standard deviations and the exact
aggregation, and states the structural feature that makes the comparison
awkward: binning by fitting patients recovers the four fitting sizes
exactly, while the other two currencies mix scenarios and sizes. A cluster
bootstrap over the sixteen scenarios supplies an interval for the
difference.

Minors. Table S1f gives the full criterion correlation matrix for each
cohort, which Table S1 had summarised by its largest off-diagonal value.
Table S1d reports how often a comparator ties the rule exactly. The
uncertainty paragraph no longer opens with an unconditional statement
about bias-corrected intervals. The RMST-rule direction is stated
unambiguously. The title takes the reviewer's wording.

Corrections found by our own audit rather than by the review:

- The cluster-bootstrap currency difference had been reported as the
  bootstrap MEAN rather than the observed difference. The quartile edges
  are recut inside each replicate, so the two disagree -- here in sign.
  The point estimates are now the observed differences, the bootstrap
  supplies only the intervals, and Table S2c states the gap.
- "Spreading the enrichment never hurts" is not what Table S4 shows: the
  effect is established at 6000 fitting patients only, and at 600 the
  point estimates run the other way.
- "The effective-sample-size fraction is flat in the number of criteria"
  is a monotone decline, small but not absent. The size of the decline
  was always the point; the text now says so.
- Table S3 gave four matching tolerances on the hazard ratio but only one
  on the restricted mean, while the text claimed both estimands at all
  four. The three missing rows are added, as is the published rule's
  probability of a greater restricted mean difference with its interval.
- The Methods said six interval procedures and the Results said four.
- The eligibility promise across the fitting-size ladder is not monotone
  and its minimum is not at the smallest rung.
- The factor by which correctly adjusted confounding raises the sample
  requirement depends on the reliability target; the target is now named.
- The provenance check's exemptions -- whitelist matching by value,
  monospaced model specifications, and figures written in words or
  exponent form -- are disclosed in the Methods and in Text S1.6 rather
  than living only in a code comment.
- The claim that a single script supplies every observed-cohort point
  estimate is scoped to the quantities it actually covers, with the other
  tables' scripts named.
- Response letters one to four now carry the current title, as five and
  six already did. Letter two reads three retired result objects; the
  values it quoted are preserved in it as literals, transcribed from the
  letter as built, so it still builds and still says what it said.
