Ninth revision: stop using the ranges for implicit null-hypothesis
inference, separate the observed range from the heuristic one, and
withdraw an explanation we invented rather than checked

The Methods had said that no conclusion turns on whether a range covers
one half or zero, and then three sentences did exactly that: the matched
comparison's ranges "straddle one half, which is the substance of what
these cohorts can say", the paired rule differences "lie on both sides of
zero", and the Conclusions used the second as part of its reason for
claiming neither advantage nor equivalence. All three are gone. What
replaces them is the width of the range, computed from the result file
rather than described in words, plus an explicit statement that no
calibrated population-level comparison was performed. The equivalence
claim now rests only on the fact that no margin was prespecified and no
equivalence test was run.

The observed and the variance-adjusted ranges are no longer joined by a
slash under one heading. Tables S3 and S3c carry five columns: the
quantity, then for each cohort the point estimate with the OBSERVED
PATIENT-RESAMPLED RANGE beneath it, and the VARIANCE-ADJUSTED SENSITIVITY
RANGE in a column of its own. The notes say which is an empirical quantile
of the resampled statistic and which is a heuristic transformation of
unknown quantile accuracy, and that the main text quotes the first and
never the second. Table S1e, which the S3 note points at, now uses the
same two names instead of a third.

On the counts: there were no failed replicates to explain. Every row of
all four nested result objects is finite. The first outer replicate uses
the unresampled cohort (b == 1) and is held out of the bootstrap
distribution as the observed-data replicate whose disagreement with the
repeated-split estimate Table S3c reports, so B - 1 resamples form each
range. The eighth revision noticed the mismatch between 150 and 149,
assumed a failure and wrote that a replicate had been dropped where the
split procedure failed. That was not checked and was untrue. The Methods
and both table notes now give the real reason and say the earlier
statement was wrong.

Wording: "separates algorithmic stability from population uncertainty"
becomes an attempted approximate decomposition, with the note that for
some quantities the patient-level component is not identifiable at this
Monte Carlo precision. The Discussion sentence on information currencies
is split in two so the withdrawal attaches only to the earlier
threshold-conditioned comparison, and "robustly" is replaced by "not
driven by any single scenario" there, in Table S2c and in the Results.

Typos: the abstract's stray punctuation after "equal weight"; and the
Table S2c bin-edge header, which printed 71 for 710 because the
significant-figure routine added in the last revision stripped trailing
zeros from the formatted string. That would have corrupted any edge ending
in zero, so it is fixed at the source rather than in the one place it
happened to show.

Found by our own audit of this redraft:

- The abstract said the paired difference was "about as sensitive to
  cohort composition as the marginal figures". It is not: the paired
  central ranges are 0.24 to 0.36 wide against 0.52 to 0.60 for the
  marginal ones, and the sentence deleted the only justification the paper
  gives for pairing.
- "About a third of the unit interval" was attached to ranges of width
  0.26 to 0.32, and "about a quarter of a unit" to paired ranges as wide
  as 0.36. Every such phrase is now an interpolated number.
- Results said the 2.5 and 97.5 points rest on the third replicate at 150
  resamples; at 149 they rest on the fourth, as the Methods already said.
- Table S1e claimed the observed-to-implied spread ratio ran from about
  0.7 to 1.0 in the well-determined columns and 0.6 to 2.5 overall; the
  table's own cells give 0.59 to 1.14 and 0.41 to 2.45. Both are now
  computed from the result file.
- Table S2c named one scenario whose omission reverses the sign of the
  events-minus-patients difference; three do.
- Figure 2's panel title still read "One promise is robust", and its
  subtitle still said "outer-bootstrap intervals". Both regenerated.
- Several smaller repairs: a dangling "neither of which" clause left by an
  earlier rewrite, a present/past tense break in the abstract and
  Conclusions, a singular antecedent taking a plural pronoun, and the
  Results describing a leave-one-out sweep as ruling out "one or two"
  scenarios when it drops one at a time.
