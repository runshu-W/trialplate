Sixth revision: patient-level uncertainty for the three-way results, paired rule comparison, and a title that matches the evidence

Two of this round's comments are the same mistake in different places: we applied a
standard to most of the paper and then exempted the numbers we had just promoted to
the abstract.

Three-way results now carry patient-level uncertainty (major 1):
analysis/threeway_nested.R (new) wraps the whole fit / select / test pipeline in the
outer bootstrap over patient identifiers, with the three-way split itself taken over
unique ids so no patient appears in more than one third. The confirmatory quantities
now have intervals: replication rate 35.3% [0.29, 0.57] colon and 41.4% [0.25, 0.54]
Rotterdam; the rule left undominated by every pre-selected subset 21.0% [0.04, 0.36]
and 12.0% [0.04, 0.36]. The comparison is now against 2.5% and 2.7% from the
SELECTION THIRD of the same design, not against the half-split 3.0% and 1.0%, which
also change the evaluation-set size and so confound two effects.

The bootstrap's own observed-data replicate differs from the point-estimate run by up
to 0.14 across these quantities. That gap is reported in the caption and the response
letter rather than left for a reader to find.

Title and Conclusions weakened (major 2):
Sixteen factorial scenarios establish heterogeneity among the mechanisms examined,
not an impossibility result. The title now reads "...and why no data requirement can
be read from cohort size alone". The Results heading, the abstract and the
Conclusions no longer assert that no unconditional requirement EXISTS, only that none
could be determined from cohort size, event count or effective sample size across the
mechanisms simulated.

Paired rule comparison (major 3):
The two rules share patients and splits, so two intervals each formed against the full
protocol cannot say which is better. nested_all.R now records the published rule's
absolute-benefit indicator, which was missing, and forms the paired difference inside
each split. In colon the RMST-selected variant's paired advantage is 0.024
[-0.200, 0.356] on the restricted mean difference and -0.138 [-0.360, 0.160] on the
hazard ratio; every interval contains zero and the paired intervals are narrower than
the marginal ones. The paper now says the paired advantage of either rule was not
established, and that no equivalence margin was prespecified.

Simulation uncertainty and the limits of the build guard (major 4):
Table S3b reports its replicate count and a Monte Carlo SE per probability; Table S4b
now carries MC SEs too. The Methods no longer claim no figure can drift: the guard
tests decimals and percentages, and 18 integer counts are asserted individually
instead, with the build stopping if any changes.
