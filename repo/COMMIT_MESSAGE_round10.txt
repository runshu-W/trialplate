Tenth revision: stop attributing the range width to cohort composition,
report the counts the ranges actually rest on, and put the usable
inner-split counts in the paper

The Methods said the observed range carries both patient-resampling
variability and the Monte Carlo noise of a 25-split estimator, and the
Results then attributed the whole width to which patients happen to be in
the cohort. Both sentences the reviewer named are rewritten, and so are the
three others that said the same thing more quietly. The Results now report
the width of the combined resampling-and-splitting procedure, state that
the width is not attributable to the patients alone, and — where the paper
does attribute something to patient sampling — point at the identifiable
outer-sampling standard deviation (0.117 and 0.090 against totals of 0.128
and 0.105 for the matched comparison) rather than at the total width. The
paired paragraph adds that the inner Monte Carlo component is a larger
share of the total for the paired quantities than for the marginal ones, so
the narrower paired widths are not patient-level variability either.

Tables S3 and S3c no longer call 150 and 100 "outer resamples". The row now
reads "bootstrap resamples x inner splits, forming the ranges" at 149 x 25
and 99 x 25, with a row beneath giving the one observed-data evaluation
held out of the ranges. Four places in the prose that still quoted the
nominal counts are corrected with it, including inside Table S3's own note.

On the unusable inner splits, the four questions have four definite
answers, and the machinery was already doing the right thing without ever
saying so. A resample's value for a statistic is the mean over the splits
that define it. The R in s squared over R is that resample's own usable
count, never the nominal one. No statistic is undefined in a whole
resample anywhere, so every resample contributes to every range, and the
export now asserts that over all four result objects unconditionally.
New Supplementary Table S1g gives the counts under the names the paper uses
for the statistics rather than their column names in the result file.

Frequency turned out to matter more than depth, and our first draft of that
table reported only the minimum and the median, which made the shortfall
look occasional. It is not. The margin-constrained three-way replication
rate is below the nominal count in 85 of 99 colon resamples and 91 of 99
Rotterdam resamples, because it needs a dominator on the selection third
that also clears the margin. The table now gives range, median and
frequency, and Table S1e's note says that sizing the inner Monte Carlo
component at the nominal count therefore understates it slightly.

New: analysis/check_outer.R, so the round-nine correction can be verified
rather than taken on trust. It reads the four stored result objects and the
analysis sources and prints, for each, the non-finite cell count, the rows
stored against the nominal replicate count, the bootstrap resamples behind
each range, and the usable inner-split counts; it computes the Monte Carlo
term both ways; and it exits non-zero on failure. The round-nine admission
takes the reviewer's phrasing: we inferred the cause incorrectly without
inspecting the stored result object, and the subsequent audit showed that
no outer replicate failed or was discarded.

Found by our own audit of this redraft:

- The first draft of check_outer.R printed two of its four statements as
  fixed text: that row one is the observed-data evaluation, and which line
  of the export sets the Monte Carlo denominator. It also never checked
  "nothing discarded" at all — B_OUT is stored after any drop, so a run
  that lost two replicates would have printed a smaller count and still
  passed. It now parses the nominal count out of the analysis script,
  compares it with the rows stored, and locates and prints the two source
  lines instead of describing them.
- The seventh export check was inside an "if the objects exist" guard, so a
  missing or older result object would have skipped it silently while the
  Methods claimed it stops the build. It is unconditional.
- Two attributions of the width to patients alone survived the first pass,
  one of them in the abstract; the letter's "Where" line pointed at a
  section that had not been touched.
- A stated width contradicted the endpoints printed beside it, because the
  width was computed from unrounded endpoints and the endpoints printed to
  two decimals.
- A helper returning a minimum and a maximum was read as "colon and
  Rotterdam" in two sentences that followed a colon/Rotterdam enumeration.
- Figure 2's caption said the ranges span more than half the unit interval;
  that is true of the effect-estimate promise and not of the eligibility
  promise plotted in the same panel.
- Text S1.6 described the primary-versus-nested agreement check as a Monte
  Carlo calculation; it is a fixed tolerance, and the note now says so and
  points at the observed gaps.
